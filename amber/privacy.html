About this policy

Updated October 16, 2026. Amber is provided by LAVAS LABS LIMITED. Contact reallayoute@gmail.com for privacy questions or requests. This policy describes the current personal beta. Amber has no team backend, gateway, advertising, cross-app tracking or proactive developer analytics uploads, and no in-app purchases or subscriptions.

Local data and credentials

Connection settings such as names, hosts, ports, usernames, database names and options are stored on your device. Saved SQL, query history and drafts may contain business information or literal values. Separate parameter inputs use driver binding and are not recorded as parameter values in query history; SQL literals you write remain part of the SQL. Recent connection/object metadata and local query folders are also stored on the device. Query-library files are excluded from system backup.

Database passwords, SSH passwords/private keys and SSH user certificates use separate system Keychain items governed by their sync controls and your iCloud Keychain settings. Database passwords are absent from connection configuration files. Proxy passwords remain device-only. Supported SSH keys can be imported from a selected file or an explicit system paste action. Encrypted-key passphrases are used locally, never saved or uploaded; credentials change only on successful import. Migration failures/conflicts retain usable local credentials and report the problem. The app cannot confirm delivery to another device.

Public keys are copied or shared only when you choose that action; private keys remain in Keychain. You and the storage provider manage original key files, clipboard content and previously shared copies. SSH certificates are public authentication material but may contain account, principal or organization information. Local SSH host fingerprints are not synchronized.

Databases and network routes you choose

Your actions send authentication information, queries, confirmed changes, import data and necessary protocol data directly to the database servers you select. Results and schema information are processed on the device. Amber does not proactively upload database content or credentials to a developer-hosted database service. Database operators and network services may log activity under their own retention policies.

TLS verifies certificates and hostnames by default. Explicitly disabling database TLS, including for PROD, removes that protocol’s TLS protection; failures never trigger an automatic downgrade. SSH and SOCKS5 are separate routes, not substitutes for database TLS. SSH servers receive hop authentication and forwarding targets; proxies receive authentication and destination metadata. Those operators may log activity. SSH records first-use fingerprints and verifies later connections, blocking changes; verify the target before first use. Supported CA file import handles selected public PEM/DER certificates and rejects private-key content.

Reviewed writes, administration and migrations send necessary values, including new database passwords you enter, to the selected database, not automatically to Amber or an AI provider. MySQL DDL may partially commit. Locking, cancellation or timeout cannot retract an already-sent write; verify unknown server outcomes before retry. MySQL/PostgreSQL read cancellation attempts a bounded server-side stop, without a guarantee. Visible operations pages release connections when leaving, backgrounding or locking PROD. Background sampling has only a short system allowance and stops when suspended or terminated.

Files, exports and copied content

The system file picker grants access to files you select. Imports parse selected content and send it to the reviewed target after your confirmation. SQLite operations can read selected files, create working copies and check before saving. Failures can retain temporary or recovery files with an in-app notice. A selected cloud file provider may manage the file’s location and copies.

CSV, JSON, SQL, Excel and image exports/shares can contain selected results, schema, query text or column comments. Amber does not automatically encrypt or redact these files. Masking rules provide local drafts/previews. You choose destinations, recipients and deletion; receiving apps and providers may retain copies.

Copying results places content on the system clipboard, with an optional expiry handled by the system. Expiry does not erase content already pasted, exported or saved elsewhere. System continuity and receiving apps follow their own settings and policies.

AI services you configure

AI is unconfigured by default. After you provide a HTTPS endpoint, model and API key and send a question, Amber authenticates with that service’s key. Explicit model-list loading also sends its key. OpenAI and Anthropic compatible protocols are supported. Requests use no persistent HTTP cache or cookies and do not forward requests through HTTP redirects. Remove the current key in AI Service settings to disable requests.

SQL generation sends your question, matching conversation history, relevant selected schema/comments and engine/time context. It does not automatically attach database result rows or login credentials. Explanation or rewriting sends the current SQL and task instructions. Questions, history, SQL literals and object names can contain sensitive business information. Charts are generated locally without uploading result rows for drawing.

Optional read-only analysis, after its on-device sharing disclosure and your question, can list tables, describe schema and request checked SELECT or ordinary EXPLAIN. Tool observations may include table/column names and comments, indexes/foreign keys, engine version, device time/time zone, bounded result previews and normalized plan summaries. A turn permits six tools and three queries; a result is capped at 50 rows, 40 columns, 512 characters per value and about 12 KB encoded, with further trimming possible. Complete or keyword-matched table inventories can contain business information. Credentials, raw server errors and raw plans are not automatic tool observations. No write, arbitrary-command or connection-switch tools are exposed.

Explicit operations-AI diagnosis can send selected slow-statement/session metrics, sample time, collection limits, normalized plan summaries and SQL after literal/comment processing. Identifiers and metrics may remain sensitive. Raw plans, remote errors, credentials and result rows are not automatically attached to that diagnosis.

Provider responses appear locally. Streaming partial SQL is view-only; tools and final SQL require complete protocol success and validation. SQL-generation suggestions and rewrites require your explicit action before running through the normal query flow. The provider may process and retain prompts, history, SQL, schema, observations and responses under its privacy, pricing and retention policies. Review those policies and business sensitivity before configuring or using the service.

Local conversations and interrupted tasks

AI questions, replies, generated SQL and connection identifiers can be retained in local protected history excluded from backup, outside query-library CloudKit sync. History remembers mode preferences, never execution grants. The first read-only sharing disclosure is acknowledged once on this device; changing connection/service/model/key does not repeat it. Runtime scope, target and lock checks still apply. Only matching connection, engine and AI-service history can become follow-up context; other history remains display-only.

Unfinished questions, received text and read-only tool checkpoints with bounded results are saved locally so an interrupted task can continue. Recovery files use complete data protection, are excluded from system backup and do not sync through iCloud. They contain no API keys or raw execution errors.

Continuing may send a new request to the matching original AI service with the question, saved text and necessary bounded observations. Saved tool results retain their original capture time. Matching steps whose successful results were saved are reused; incomplete reads are revalidated before retry. Unknown outcomes cannot be promised exactly once. Changes to connection configuration or AI service invalidate old recovery progress. A short iOS background allowance may let a request continue; expiry, locking or disconnection may pause it until you return and choose Continue. Continuous background execution is not guaranteed.

Task completion removes temporary recovery records. Result values included in an AI answer may remain in ordinary conversation history. You can view and delete local conversations from history. Deleting local content cannot remove data already received by an AI provider.

Connection profiles and iCloud

After verifying the current Apple account and enabling connection sync, Apple iCloud KVS can synchronize names, hosts, ports, usernames, database options, stable UUIDs, non-credential SSH/proxy metadata, revisions and deletion markers. Supported public CA DER may travel in V2 profiles; certificate organization metadata may contain business information. Review before enabling.

Existing profiles require first-time confirmation under My → iCloud Sync; an empty new device can receive after account verification. Connection-profile account verification obtains a container-scoped anonymous CloudKit identifier; its digest stays local and profile data travels through KVS. Unverified accounts do not upload. Account changes pause sync until explicitly enabled. Offline edits wait; conflicts retain versions for selection. Received profiles do not connect automatically; new/restored targets require credential confirmation.

Passwords, private keys, local SSH fingerprints, SQLite files/bookmarks, query content/results, caches and AI keys are excluded from profile sync. System iCloud Keychain handles credentials separately. Disabling profile sync does not automatically erase submitted cloud profiles; profile deletion does not delete database-server records. Local submit/receive status is not confirmation that another device received data.

Query, folder and preference sync

Saved queries and query history have separate, initially disabled switches. Once enabled and account-verified, they write to your Apple account’s private CloudKit database. SQL, saved titles and connection display names use encrypted fields; UUIDs, dates, counts, durations, row counts and failure flags are ordinary metadata. Review business information before enabling. Result rows, editor drafts, passwords, private keys and local SSH fingerprints are excluded.

Whitelisted appearance/editor/notification preferences can separately use Apple iCloud KVS. The whitelist includes AI protocol, HTTPS endpoint, model and row-card/table display preferences. A received AI configuration requires local review and confirmation before requests. Unknown initial cloud settings wait for explicit confirmation; choosing local settings can replace the cloud snapshot. Separate folder KVS sync contains folder names, saved-query UUID membership, revisions and deletion markers, without SQL.

Settings and folders verify and bind the Apple account before reads/writes. Account changes pause the corresponding sync controls until explicitly enabled. Disabling does not automatically erase submitted records; enabling again reconciles known deletion intent and local edits, and conflicts may retain edits. Lock, PROD verification, background/recording privacy, clipboard-expiry and Handoff-broadcast settings remain device-specific. Real two-device KVS/Keychain delivery, account switching and deletion propagation remain unverified.

AI keys and system Keychain

AI keys are device-only by default in a separate system Keychain service, isolated by HTTPS endpoint and protocol. A separate, initially disabled device switch permits sending/receiving through system iCloud Keychain without changing database/SSH credential controls. Keys are absent from configuration files, query history and logs. Key or configuration changes invalidate old AI-session scopes.

Disabling AI-key sync attempts to preserve a usable local copy before removing matching cloud data. Failures retain data and report an error; this does not prove every device’s copy was deleted. AI-session and migration-ownership digests stay in a ThisDeviceOnly Keychain service, not configuration files or logs. System Keychain delivery and retention follow your Apple account and system settings.

Voice, permissions and device protection

The in-app microphone requires microphone permission and uses Apple Speech for on-device transcription. System language assets may need downloading. Audio stays in memory, without recording files or upload to Amber or your AI provider. Interim text is preview-only. Cancel preserves the original draft; Finish appends final text to an editable draft. Manual Send then shares the text through the existing AI flow. Leaving, backgrounding, locking, changing conversation/service or opening an overlay cancels voice input.

Local-network permission supports LAN connections. App lock uses system Face ID or device-passcode verification; Amber does not receive biometric templates or your passcode. Camera access is for on-device QR scanning, not saving/uploading photos. Notifications are local/test notifications, without a connected team push service; notification text is generic and excludes SQL, results and connection names.

The app accesses preferences for settings, selected-file metadata to check changes and free disk space before writes. This information supports local features, not fingerprinting or advertising.

Handoff and Shortcuts

Non-PROD system Handoff is initially enabled and can be disabled. While unlocked, it can share a connection UUID and bounded SQL up to 3 KiB, or database/table object names, to your Apple devices, without hosts, credentials or result rows. SQL and names may contain business information. Receipt does not execute SQL. Saved-query Shortcuts retain read-only and device-verification limits. Local status does not prove actual cross-device delivery.

Support, Apple services and deletion

Help does not automatically send feedback. If you contact support, we use the email, description and redacted attachments you provide to handle the request. Keep credentials and production data out of reports. Support information is retained as needed for the request and applicable record-keeping; contact reallayoute@gmail.com to request access, correction or deletion.

Apple processes TestFlight installation, usage diagnostics, crashes and beta feedback according to its policies and your settings and may share reports with the developer. Email, sharing, file providers, backups, iCloud and Keychain follow their respective policies and settings.

You can delete local connections and their associated credentials, remove saved queries, clear query history, delete AI conversations, remove a service key or revoke system permissions. Amber does not delete data already held by a database server or AI provider. Syncable Keychain deletion and retention are handled by the system. Temporary files, recovery copies and exports follow the relevant feature and storage location. Other local files are not all excluded from backup. Uninstalling does not guarantee removal of Keychain entries, backups, cloud data or files you saved elsewhere.

We will update this policy and its date when data handling changes.
